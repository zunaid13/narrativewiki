**Recommendation: substantial revision before submission as an ACL research paper.** The task and artifact-level threat model are useful, and the negative findings deserve publication if validated. The present evidence is an agent-annotated pilot on one development series. Unvalidated labels, a disclosure measure with unmeasured sensitivity, mismatched output populations, and concrete uncertainty/cost reporting inconsistencies prevent a strong acceptance recommendation. I do not object to the paper because NarrativeWiki loses to simpler baselines; those losses are informative. I object to treating this evaluation as secure evidence for the broader conclusions.

The provisional scores use the categories in the [official ARR review form](https://github.com/acl-org/aclrollingreview/blob/main/reviewform.md): soundness **2/5**, excitement **3.5/5**, overall assessment **2/5** (resubmit next cycle), reviewer confidence **4/5**, and reproducibility **3/5** from the manuscript as supplied. The repository improves inspectability, but the PDF does not provide an accessible anonymous artifact location. These are subjective review judgments, not empirical measurements. The intended contribution is promising enough to justify revision.

**Paper summary.** NarrativeWiki extracts evidence-bearing character facts from a novel series, constructs a graph with disclosure-volume intervals, and publishes a linked reference for each reading cutoff. It distinguishes reading order from story chronology and considers construction dependence, parametric knowledge, and disclosure through the delivered site. The study evaluates the first five publication-order Anne books against prefix long-context generation, prefix hybrid retrieval, a LightRAG adaptation, a full-graph diagnostic, closed-book generation, and full-text generation instructed to respect a cutoff. The proposed system approximately matches long-context recall, produces substantially more assertions, and has no confirmed disclosure of the assessed future gold facts. It has lower page-level precision and much weaker paragraph citation sufficiency. Prefix-versus-full graph comparisons also reveal identities whose early pages depend on later volumes.

**Strengths worth preserving.** The application is understandable and useful: a reference that changes with the reader's progress solves a concrete problem. Reading-order cutoffs are a better formulation than story-time filtering for this application. The threat model goes beyond generated prose to titles, addresses, navigation, backlinks, source views, and shared pages. The interval-end masking and per-quote volume checks address subtle disclosure paths. Comparisons share a generator, and the strong long-context baseline prevents an easy but misleading graph-versus-weak-retrieval victory. The paper reports unfavorable precision and citation outcomes instead of hiding them. Its labeling audits, casebook, prompts, seeds, per-cutoff tables, and provenance design make errors unusually inspectable. The distinction between supported truth and sufficient citations is also useful. These strengths should remain in a revised paper.

**Major concern 1: the evaluation lacks independent validated ground truth.** Section 5, pp. 5–6, lines 359–389, and Limitations, p. 9, explicitly state that all gold facts and labels were produced by unblinded LLM agents; one agent helped build the system. The audits changed 108 recall decisions and corrected 34 comparison-system precision labels. Both moved against NarrativeWiki. Transparency about this is excellent, but auditing the same agents does not establish the reliability of the remaining positives, negatives, reveal dates, or inferred disclosures. There is also an annotator confound: a different agent labels NarrativeWiki precision at early cutoffs but not matched baseline cells. A model name and a source-reading instruction are not a human validation protocol.

The necessary improvement is a blinded, independently double-annotated human subset, balanced across systems, cutoffs, surfaces, positive/negative labels, and uncertain disclosures. Annotators must receive eligible source context and the actual page context; randomized identifiers alone cannot hide visibly different templates. Report recruitment, familiarity with the books, instructions, time, compensation where applicable, agreement, adjudication, and agent-versus-human error rates by label and system. Include already-supported labels in the audit. The released blinded sheet is useful preparation, but releasing it is not evidence that validation happened. If human annotation cannot be obtained, consistently frame the paper as a pilot and narrow its claims.

**Major concern 2: the strongest spoiler-safety conclusion exceeds the evidence.** The abstract, pp. 1–2, Section 6.1, p. 6, and Discussion/Conclusion, p. 8, move from zero detected disclosures in a selected probe set to formulations such as the boundary holding when enforced by input. The paper itself acknowledges that candidate retrieval can miss a paraphrase and that the generator recognizes the work. Therefore zero found future gold facts is not a guarantee of safety, a demonstrated noninterference property, or a causal proof that bounded input suffices. The same pretrained model can access later facts through its parameters even when supplied only early text. Pooling fact-by-cutoff reads across systems does not create independent replications.

Use an empirical formulation: in these runs, systems receiving eligible passages or facts produced no confirmed disclosure of the assessed future gold facts. Report confirmed, uncertain, and lexical findings together. Distinguish input eligibility, factual disclosure, identity dependence, and a hypothetical stronger safety guarantee. The current candid limitation paragraph should constrain the abstract and conclusion, rather than leave readers to reconcile their stronger wording themselves.

**Major concern 3: disclosure detection has unmeasured recall.** Section 5, p. 5, lines 347–358, reads retrieved candidates, not every sentence against every future fact. Appendix B, p. 11, lines 854–856, even allows “absent” when no candidate is found. The missed “marry” case in Section 6.1 is direct evidence that the search has false negatives. A lexical screen over later names is complementary, but cannot validate disclosures about already-known characters, unnamed events, numerical details, or indirect paraphrases. Sharing the same search across systems does not eliminate differential sensitivity when systems produce different styles, amounts, and surfaces.

Separate no-candidate from reviewed-and-not-disclosed labels. Measure candidate recall on seeded disclosures that cover direct wording, paraphrases, implications, aliases, titles, links, relation qualifiers, and source views; preserve benign eligible controls. Have an independent reader inspect a random sample of output regions that generated no candidate. Report detection sensitivity by surface and disclosure type. Distinguish actual later information from a merely plausible genre prediction. A finite future-gold set should remain an explicitly bounded measure.

**Major concern 4: construction dependence is important but is not measured by the headline metric.** Section 6.3, pp. 7–8, reports later-anchored early pages while M4 remains zero. The Walter example is particularly valuable: a future identity changes an early reference even without stating an annotated future proposition. That is evidence for a separate failure mode, not evidence that a full-series build is completely cutoff-safe. Comparing only pages present in one build misses changes to canonical names, links, qualifiers, evidence selection, and prose on shared pages.

Give construction dependence its own outcome and audit shared pages as well as page-set differences. A useful proposed criterion is that changing the suffix after a fixed prefix should not alter the reader-visible artifact at that cutoff, subject to a declared equivalence relation. This is a proposed project experiment, not an established result here. Control generation variance through deterministic shared stages or preserved prompt/response replay. Also soften the claim on p. 8 that avoiding dependence necessarily requires a full build per cutoff: temporally versioned identity decisions or incremental prefix processing are plausible alternatives, although they have not been tested in this project.

**Major concern 5: systems do not actually share the evaluated output population.** Section 5, p. 4, lines 304–310, and Appendix E, p. 13, say the systems share cast, schema, and output budget. Yet baselines generate a selected gold cast, while NarrativeWiki discovers additional characters and publishes pair, codex, and timeline pages. Table 7 alone reports 133 character pages for the full build at cutoff two, compared with the baseline cast rule's 21 pages. Section 6.2 explicitly says comparison systems lack pair pages. Thus precision, total output, and citation coverage estimate different mixtures of content. The common character-page template is a useful control, but not a common whole-artifact schema.

Describe both populations explicitly. Report a matched comparison on common character pages and shared fields, alongside the product-level evaluation of each complete artifact. Break out precision, citation coverage, and supported output by surface. If the extra linked reference is a central contribution, either give a simple baseline a comparable deterministic artifact wrapper or demonstrate the extra product's utility. Do not remove whole-artifact errors to improve a score; make both comparisons visible.

**Major concern 6: “precision” combines factual support with page relevance.** Section 6.2, p. 6, lines 462–474, explains the gap between page-level precision and atom-only support through pair pages containing scenes involving either person. A true statement about one character is not automatically a false claim just because it appears on a pair page. It is misleading if the heading, layout, or surrounding prose implies that both characters participated. The evaluation needs to establish that implication from the rendered artifact. Conversely, genuine wrong-subject attribution is a factual support error.

Retain the user-facing failure but separate atom support, attribution/frame correctness, and page relevance or misleading placement. Give actual rendered examples, not only sampled atom text, and a decision rule distinguishing a clearly labeled individual history from a purported joint scene. Blinded page judgments or a small reader check would strengthen this distinction. Avoid implying that the lower precision is an unavoidable price of breadth when the described bug may be repaired deterministically.

**Major concern 7: citation-interval reporting is concretely inconsistent.** Table 1, p. 7, and Section 5 describe intervals on means as character-clustered bootstraps. Recomputing pooled Wilson intervals from the stored M8 labels gives B5 22/197 → [0.075, 0.163], B1 130/199 → [0.585, 0.716], and B2 119/159 → [0.676, 0.809], exactly the printed M8 intervals. The observation-backed `paper_numbers.json` contains no M8 entries. This is a confirmed reporting mismatch, not a guess about statistical implementation.

Choose and document the estimand. For an unweighted cutoff mean, generate uncertainty using an appropriate design that retains repeated character/cutoff observations and the sampling structure. If reporting a pooled rate with Wilson intervals, label it separately and state the independence approximation. Do not attach pooled intervals to a differently defined macro mean without explanation. Supply a small numeric-to-source audit for every table column. The very similar point estimates do not resolve the uncertainty mismatch.

**Major concern 8: some comparative language treats inconclusive estimates as equivalence.** “Matches,” “ties,” “do not differ,” and “comparable” appear across the abstract and pp. 6–8. A paired recall difference of −0.002 with interval [−0.100, 0.054] does not establish practical equivalence. Nor do near-equal among-cited precision point estimates with very different sample sizes. The uncertainty excludes neither some meaningful disadvantages nor some advantages. Section 7 also uses overlap of intervals as support for comparability.

Use “similar point estimates; the interval includes zero” unless an equivalence/noninferiority margin was specified and tested. Report common-cutoff comparisons for incomplete systems. Define whether an interval targets a fixed artifact's sampled assertions, variation over characters, or variation over regenerated systems; current intervals do not capture model randomness or annotation errors. State precisely how multi-character pages are clustered. The exact zero-event bound based on character count should be labeled as a character-level risk bound, with its independent-unit assumptions, rather than silently attached to a fact-level rate.

**Major concern 9: cost accounting is not comparable for LightRAG.** Table 1 calls cost a clean build of all five cutoffs and prints B3 at $3.11. Appendix G, p. 14, calls that value LightRAG's page-call cost. The stored cost file confirms $3.11034975 under `baseline_page` and contains no `baseline_lightrag` stage, although the adapter and Appendix E say index extraction uses the same paid generator. NarrativeWiki's total includes discovery, extraction, arbitration, verification, and prose. This omission can mislead a reader comparing architectures. No corrected LightRAG total was computed during this review.

Recover index-construction costs from historical call records, distinguish initial index, incremental indexing, page generation, retries, and cached replay, and compare the same workload. A query-only successful rerun can supersede the construction run under the current final-run selection logic; audit that possibility rather than assuming the latest run reconstructs an existing index. Separate standardized token-price estimates from recorded spend, and state whether provider prompt caching/batch/Flex discounts are included. Explain incomplete B2 coverage in the cost column as well as in recall. For accessibility, report wall-clock time and hardware for local components where available.

**Major concern 10: the corpus is both a development set and a memorized work.** The paper properly discloses this, but a selected gold inventory from one famous series cannot establish generalization. The closed-book diagnostic's substantial recall demonstrates that source-based recovery is not isolated. There is also a conceptual mismatch between a broadly useful reader service and an evaluation deliberately limited to a work familiar to the generator.

Add a genuinely held-out work after freezing the pipeline and checking development history; keep any future processing within the maintainer's approved corpus/volume scope. A transformation of existing text can be an additional control, but name replacement or paraphrasing does not prove decontamination and can damage facts. Validate treatment fidelity and recognition reduction separately. If these experiments are unavailable, narrow the empirical contribution to this pilot and treat external validation as unfinished research. I would not demand all models, genres, and languages before publication; one credible independent evaluation would address the most immediate generalization concern.

**Major concern 11: the claimed graph benefit needs stronger measurement.** Zero M4 disclosure is shared with simpler prefix systems, recall does not improve over long-context generation, page precision is worse, and citation sufficiency is much worse. The remaining benefit is a reusable linked artifact and more content. Additional assertions and pages are not intrinsically useful: duplicated scenes, isolated minor entities, and noisy identities can make reference use harder. A fixed gold inventory also does not measure whether the additional content is valuable.

Frame the contribution around artifact construction, an evaluation protocol, and observed tradeoffs. Measure useful supported content on deduplicated semantic propositions, coverage across characters, navigation success, redundancy, and source-checking burden. A small task study could ask readers to find relationships or verify a fact while staying at their cutoff. A reader study is useful if the paper makes usability claims; it need not be an additional mandatory experiment for a narrowly framed systems/evaluation paper. Avoid multiplying sampled occurrence-weighted precision by a distinct-text count and calling the result supported unique content without a compatible sampling design.

**Major concern 12: component evidence is too weak to attribute the findings to the architecture.** The extraction passes, verifier, name merging, interval construction, and publication rules are all plausible. However, a verifier's high accuracy on minimally edited facts does not establish reliable validation of real extraction errors, particularly when the harder nested-story examples fail. A same-model generator/verifier can also share error modes. A literal quotation check establishes that a string exists, not that the asserted fact follows from it.

Validate the verifier on sampled real admitted and rejected claims, including hypothetical speech, beliefs, negation, namesakes, and narrative frame. Independently validate challenge-set labels. Start with stored-prompt and stored-claim ablations of the verifier, alias policy, and extraction passes. Report which changes affect precision, recall, and yield, rather than introducing every possible ablation. Cheap deterministic artifact corrections can be compared on existing outputs; there is no methodological reason to hide or silently overwrite the frozen original when showing a clearly separated repaired condition.

**Major concern 13: temporal and epistemic assumptions need sharper definitions.** Section 4 treats conflicting single-valued attributes in one volume as extraction errors, but a character can age, change occupation, marry, or change status within one volume. Different-volume contradiction can also be an error or revised belief rather than legitimate story change. The system's first evidence volume may be the earliest extracted supporting passage, while gold reveal volume is defined as the earliest sufficient statement in the text. These are different quantities unless their alignment is checked. Nested stories and inferred facts further complicate the distinction between what is narrated, what a character believes, and what a reader can establish.

State the policy for within-volume transitions, retrospective references, immutable attributes, denials, presumed facts, and attributed beliefs. Declare whether cutoff means finished volume; the limitation about readers not reaching an early revelation is a restriction of the user interface, rather than an error at an end-of-volume cutoff. Date identity equivalence separately from surface-name occurrence if identity safety is a claim. Algorithm 1 should operate on a local view rather than appearing to mutate graph intervals in place, and should specify what happens when all eligible evidence is removed.

FActScore, FABLES, literary coreference, and spoiler-detection work are the highest-priority additions. BooookScore/SNaC are useful if coherence/navigation quality becomes an evaluated outcome; do not expand the bibliography simply to increase its length. The cited NS-ST-GraphRAG and SearchWiki are conspicuously absent from the closest-work matrix: the former is close to temporal subgraph retrieval, and the latter constructs a linked wiki. Add them to Table 2 and distinguish the product, temporal coordinate, construction scope, and evaluation unit. Their primary abstracts were checked: [NS-ST-GraphRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05139), [SearchWiki](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29953). Those descriptions justify closer comparison, not an assertion that either already solves this exact task.

STORYSUMM's below-chance MiniCheck result is supported by its PDF tables; that citation is not the problem. However, STORYSUMM advocates multiple ways to establish narrative faithfulness and identifies limitations even in human annotations. It supports a more careful validation protocol, not substituting unvalidated source-reading agents for human grounding. Its evaluation sections and relevant result tables were checked: [STORYSUMM](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.557/).

**Reproducibility and ethical assessment.** The public-domain evaluation, explicit exclusion of development commercial-series numbers, disclosure of AI assistance, and absence of human-participant claims are appropriate. I see no specific reason to request a specialized ethics review based on the PDF. Keep the focus on artifact provenance, release licenses, faithful representation of agent versus human annotation, and reader expectations about spoiler safety. If a human validation/study is added, document its recruitment and treatment then. The paper should provide an actual anonymous release path, rather than only saying it releases everything. Cached outputs make fixed-artifact verification possible; they do not make fresh generation identical or eliminate provider-version dependence. The claim that a model alias serves one revision should be evidenced by recorded response metadata or weakened.

**Questions for the authors.**

1. What independent evidence validates the gold facts, earliest reveal volumes, and remaining supported labels after the two biased annotation passes?
2. How often does candidate retrieval recover known spoilers that avoid later names or overlap words, and how often does a blind reader find a disclosure outside the candidate set?
3. Which parts of the observed differences remain when the same characters, fields, page types, and cutoffs are compared?
4. Are M8 intervals intended to be pooled Wilson intervals or character-clustered intervals on cutoff means?
5. What is the complete cold-build cost of LightRAG, including indexing, and what workloads are included in every cost-table row?
6. How much suffix-induced change occurs on pages shared by the full and prefix builds, and how is it separated from stochastic generation?
7. Does page-level unsupported mean an actual false implication from the layout, a relevance error, or factual non-entailment?
8. What is the evidence that the extra linked content helps readers enough to justify its precision, citation, and construction-dependence costs?

**Most efficient revision order.** First correct the confirmed reporting contradictions, cost scope, population descriptions, and overstrong claim language using existing artifacts. Then validate labels and disclosure-search sensitivity before generating a larger benchmark. Recompute uncertainties and matched comparisons from the validated stored outputs. Freeze the revised protocol before any held-out experiment. Finally improve figure legibility and add the targeted related work. The outcome could be a strong task/evaluation or empirical systems paper, including negative findings, once readers can trust the measurements. Merely adding more generated pages or more model runs will not resolve the central concerns.
