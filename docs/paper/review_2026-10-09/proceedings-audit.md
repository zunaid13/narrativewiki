## What should and should not be changed

No materially mistaken BibTeX entry requires replacement in this subset. In particular, do not change `Koh, Pang` to `Koh, Pang Wei` or `Hao, Moo` to a different spelling as an alleged correction: the supplied forms are exactly what the canonical publisher exports use. This audit does not adjudicate differences between an author's usual public name and the name chosen in a publication record.

The NoCha subtitle is capitalized differently from the Anthology export. It is a cosmetic normalization, not a fabricated title or wrong paper. To match the canonical BibTeX literally, its title field can be:

```bibtex
title = {One Thousand and One Pairs: A ``novel'' challenge for long-context language models},
```

The Emperor entry uses an ASCII apostrophe where PMLR uses a typographic apostrophe; this is also cosmetic. Optional missing editors, month ranges, ISBN, or abstract fields should not be counted as wrong metadata.

## Selected citation-context checks

The LitBank characterization in Related Work is supported by [the paper's §4.2.5](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.6.pdf): identity is annotated using information available anywhere in the text, rather than the reader's knowledge at the particular mention. This is a fair contrast to a prefix-scoped identity representation.

The STORYSUMM discussion's below-chance result is supported by [Tables 7–8 of that paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.557.pdf), but the metric and variant should be stated accurately: this concerns their Flan-T5 MiniCheck implementation and balanced accuracy, not an intrinsic property of every MiniCheck model or setting. The paper also supports the claim that any single human annotation protocol can miss errors.

The CHIRON row in Appendix R is supported by [its introduction and §6](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.499.pdf): character sheets are built up to the snippet used for masked-character prediction. Its aggregation concatenates validated per-snippet answers, and its Character-Summary baseline consumes the story prefix. Therefore CHIRON is a plausible nearby representation-and-verification comparison. It does not produce a published reader-facing wiki or evaluate artifact-wide spoiler leakage. An adapted prefix-scoped CHIRON baseline with the same backbone, roster, evidence, rendering, and scoring would strengthen the comparison; explaining why such an adaptation is infeasible is a weaker fallback. This is a reviewer concern about empirical completeness, not a bibliographic error.
