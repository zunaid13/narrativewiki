\subsection{Reading versus recall: the renamed-text control}
\label{sec:results-decon}

\paragraph{Why a control is needed.}
Given only the title and a character's name, the generator recovers
\respt{M1-anne-avg-X1} of the eligible gold facts (\respt{M1-anne-t1-X1} at $t=1$), states
\res{M4-anne-total-X1} assessed later facts and reaches sampled precision
\respt{M3-anne-avg-X1} against text it never received.

% The recall-change figure (fig:deconrecall) is printed in appendix/i-contamination.tex in v2.
\paragraph{Manipulation check.}
On 200 aligned paragraphs, title identification falls from \res{MEM-title-orig} to
\res{MEM-title-remap} after renaming alone and to \res{MEM-title-decon} after paraphrase;
masked-name restoration falls from \res{MEM-cloze-orig} to \res{MEM-cloze-decon}
(Table~\ref{tab:recognition}). Renaming alone, a common control
\citep{gupta2025whodunit,zhang2024infinitebench}, leaves the author identifiable in
\res{MEM-author-remap} of paragraphs, and paraphrase in \res{MEM-author-decon}: the prose
carries the work, not only its names.

\paragraph{Recall without text collapses; recall from text survives.}
Closed-book recall falls from \respt{M1-anne-avg-X1} to \respt{M1-annedecon-avg-X1}; its
single remaining match (\res{M1-annedecon-count-X1}) is a death that also occurs in the
generator's invented story. Every system given text changes far less
(Table~\ref{tab:decon}; Figure~\ref{fig:deconrecall}): B1 by \res{M1-annedecon-diff-B1},
\sys by \res{M1-annedecon-diff-B5}, at most \res{M1-annedecon-maxloss} (X2). This bounds
title-and-name recall on the treated text without apportioning original-text scores.
Without recognition the closed-book generator still writes full, invented pages:
\res{M3-annedecon-unsup-X1} of 200 sampled assertions are unsupported (precision
\respt{M3-anne-avg-X1} falls to \respt{M3-annedecon-avg-X1}; Appendix~\ref{app:contamination}).

\paragraph{What the original text hides.}
Three behaviors appear or worsen only under the control. \emph{The cutoff instruction:}
X2 discloses \res{M4-annedecon-total-X2} assessed later facts on treated text against
\res{M4-anne-total-X2} on the original (unique facts: \res{M4U-annedecon-X2} against
\res{M4U-anne-X2}; Table~\ref{tab:disclosure-units}), most at $t=1$
(\res{M4-annedecon-t1-X2}, against \res{M4-anne-t1-X2}); two treated-text assessments
come from a namesake created by the name map (Appendix~\ref{app:contamination}).
Familiar-text compliance thus overstated the instruction's reliability.
\emph{Long-context citations:} B1's precision holds (\respt{M3-annedecon-avg-B1} versus
\respt{M3-anne-avg-B1}), but its citation sufficiency falls from \respt{M8-anne-avg-B1}
to \respt{M8-annedecon-avg-B1}, and from \respt{M8-annedecon-t1-B1} at $t=1$ to
\respt{M8-annedecon-t4-B1} at $t=4$, as citations keep a paragraph number and lose its
volume. \emph{The probe's modality error} is described next.

\subsection{Disclosure of later facts}
\label{sec:results-main}

\paragraph{Prefix-bounded generation.}
B1, B2 and B3 each have no stated or implied disclosure in 220 fact--cutoff assessments
on either text (Figure~\ref{fig:systems}; Tables~\ref{tab:main} and~\ref{tab:decon}); B4
has none on the original.

\paragraph{The probe.}
\sys has one implied disclosure on the original text: at $t=3$ a relationship summary
says a friend \emph{later serves as a bridesmaid for Anne}, implying Anne's wedding, where
the eligible passage makes Anne the friend's bridesmaid. On treated text, \sys lists the
two leads as spouses at $t=2$ and $t=3$, a book-four fact (\res{M4-annedecon-total-B5}:
reciprocal facts at two cutoffs from one mechanism; Table~\ref{tab:disclosure-units}). The
cited evidence is a neighbor's prediction in book two; extraction stored a presumed
marriage, and deterministic rendering printed presumed spouses as spouses. B4, generating
from the same graph, lists the pair as spouses qualified as an ``anticipated future
marriage'', without the speaker (\res{M4-annedecon-total-B4}, implied). The original build
did not extract this relation, so the passage does not force the error; once stored, the
claim keeps a coarse \emph{presumed} flag but not who spoke or that it was a prediction.
A volume filter checks when evidence appears, not what it licenses.

\paragraph{Detection coverage.}
Counting uncertain verdicts raises the original-text count of B1--B5 from
\res{M4-anne-total-cut} to \res{M4-anne-total-cut-incl} assessments (\sys: five;
Appendix~\ref{app:additional}). On seeded disclosures that name the character, the
candidate search retrieves \respt{SEED-para} of paraphrases and \respt{SEED-oblique} of
implying sentences, and fewer when only a pronoun refers to the character
(Table~\ref{tab:seeds}). In the two complementary checks, no sampled assertion at $t\le4$ in
\sys or B1--B3 is established only by later volumes (\sys: \res{M5-anne-B5}). Disclosure
counts exclude identity effects (Section~\ref{sec:results-scope}).

\subsection{What decomposition gains: pages that accumulate}
\label{sec:results-gains}

Comparison systems rewrite every page at every cutoff in one call under a shared sentence
budget (Appendix~\ref{app:baselines}); \sys renders every eligible claim, so Anne
Shirley's page grows from \res{ANNEW-t1-B5} words at $t=1$ to \res{ANNEW-t5-B5} at $t=5$

\paragraph{Central characters.}
In an exploratory split on the three characters with the most gold assessments, \sys has the highest recall of
any system on treated text (\respt{CEN-annedecon-B5}, against B1's
\respt{CEN-annedecon-B1}) and, by a smaller margin, on the original (\respt{CEN-anne-B5}
against \respt{CEN-anne-B1}), where the lead comes from Anne Shirley alone

\res{CEN-annedecon-t1-B5} at $t=1$ to \res{CEN-annedecon-t5-B5} at $t=5$, while B1's falls
from \res{CEN-annedecon-t1-B1} to \res{CEN-annedecon-t5-B1}.

\paragraph{Facts kept as the reader advances.}
A fact conveyed at $t$ and lost at $t+1$ occurs once for \sys on each text; every other
system loses \res{DROP-anne-B2}--\res{DROP-anne-B3} on the original and
\res{DROP-annedecon-X2}--\res{DROP-annedecon-B2} on treated text. B4 reads the same graph
but writes each page in one call and loses \res{DROP-anne-B4} and \res{DROP-annedecon-B4}:
the stability comes from rendering stored claims, not from the graph alone.

\paragraph{Reading and breadth.}
On treated text \sys's recall is close to long context's (\respt{M1-annedecon-avg-B5}
versus \respt{M1-annedecon-avg-B1}), with the lowest spread over cutoffs
($\pm$\res{M1-annedecon-sd-B5}); it is the only system whose recall did not fall under
treatment (\res{M1-annedecon-diff-B5}). On the original text, one build publishes a mean of
\res{ND-anne-avg-B5} textually distinct assertions per cutoff, against at most
\res{ND-anne-avg-B1} for B1--B4, at
\res{UPS-anne-B5} input tokens each (B1: \res{UPS-anne-B1}; B2: \res{UPS-anne-B2}).

\subsection{What decomposition costs: coverage, support and citations}
\label{sec:results-quality}
\paragraph{Coverage.}
The central-character gain does not carry to the average: on the original text \sys
trails long context at every cutoff (\respt{M1-anne-avg-B5} versus \respt{M1-anne-avg-B1};
Figure~\ref{fig:cutoffplot}) and prefix retrieval (paired difference
\respt{M1-anne-avg-B5-minus-B2}; Table~\ref{tab:paired}), which uses a tenth of the input
tokens. The deficit lies with minor characters (Table~\ref{tab:gains}).

\paragraph{Support depends on where a claim is placed.}
Whole-artifact precision is \respt{M3-anne-avg-B5} for \sys (\res{REV-M3L} under the
stricter line rule; \res{REV-M3A} as statements alone), versus
\respt{M3-anne-avg-B1}, \respt{M3-anne-avg-B2} and \respt{M3-anne-avg-B3} for B1--B3.
On cast character pages, the matched population, \sys reaches \res{M3K-anne-B5-cast},
versus B1's pooled \res{M3K-anne-B1}. The gap concentrates on relationship pages
(\res{M3K-anne-B5-rel-share} of its assertions): \res{M3K-anne-B5-rel} are supported in
page context but \res{M3K-anne-B5-rel-atom} as statements alone, because scene lines naming
both characters reach the pair's page even when the two share no episode
(Table~\ref{tab:kinds}).

\paragraph{Citations, and a control on the same graph.}
Citation sufficiency is \respt{M8-anne-avg-B5} for \sys against \respt{M8-anne-avg-B1}
(B1) and \respt{M8-anne-avg-B2} (B2), and \res{M8C-anne-B5} against \res{M8C-anne-B1} and
\res{M8C-anne-B2} on sampled assertions that carry a citation, so omission does not
explain the gap. B4, which writes cast pages from facts retrieved from \sys's graph, has
lower recall (\respt{M1-anne-avg-B4}) but the highest original-text precision and citation
sufficiency (\respt{M3-anne-avg-B4}, \respt{M8-anne-avg-B4}): the graph's evidence can
support precise, well-cited pages, so much of the gap arises where claims are selected,
published and cited (Appendix~\ref{app:additional}). The
reported \sys is a second build; the first, before a revision made after its labeling,
had citation sufficiency \res{REV-M8-first} (Table~\ref{tab:builds}).

\subsection{Identity depends on later volumes}
\label{sec:results-scope}
Graphs built with the first build's code from volumes $1..2$ and $1..3$ disclosed no
assessed later fact and came close to the full build's recall and precision
(Table~\ref{tab:scope}), but their entities differ: of \res{A1-t2-onesided} character
pages present in only one build at $t=2$, \res{A1-t2-anchored} are anchored to later
characters (Appendix~\ref{app:casebook}).

For the reported build, identity dependence against prefix-discovered indexes is
\res{ID-anne-t1-rate} at $t=1$ and \res{ID-anne-t4-rate} at $t=4$: no prefix-built
character has exactly the page entity's set of names observed by $t$. Excluding
alias-only differences, \respt{ID-anne-strict-t1} and \respt{ID-anne-strict-t4} of pages
are regrouped or have no prefix counterpart (Table~\ref{tab:identity}). Wikis built on
those prefix indexes match the full build's pooled recall (\res{PX-anne-M1-pooled} each,
$t=1..4$) and state no later fact, though both keep the implied bridesmaid disclosure at
$t=3$ (Table~\ref{tab:prefixv2}): prefix-built indexes remove the dependence at no cost in
pooled recall. Early-volume prompts also expose later aliases
(Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}), a channel these comparisons do not isolate.
