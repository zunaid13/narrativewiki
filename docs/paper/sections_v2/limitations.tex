\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Scale.}
The evaluation covers one English public-domain series of five volumes and one generator,
with one build per system and text condition, so run-to-run variance is unknown; cutoffs
share characters and evidence and are not independent samples. Precision and citation
sufficiency rest on 40 sampled assertions per cell. All five volumes fit in the generator's
context window, which favors direct prompting; longer series, other genres, languages and
generators are untested.

\paragraph{Development on the evaluated series.}
The Anne series informed development and is not held out. The reported \sys is the second
of two builds, revised after the first was labeled (Table~\ref{tab:builds}); the baselines,
configured on another novel, were not retuned. The prefix-index wikis of
Section~\ref{sec:results-scope} were built on the original text only and labeled in one pass.

\paragraph{Annotation.}
The finalizing annotator, Claude Opus~5.5, knew which system produced each output and
assisted in building the system. The human relabeling covers original-text gold, recall,
precision and citation labels; disclosure and treated-text labels have no human check, and
human agreement is lowest for precision and citation sufficiency
(Table~\ref{tab:human-agreement}). Some gold evidence paragraphs establish their facts only
in part (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

\paragraph{Disclosure measurement.}
Disclosure is assessed for a selected inventory of later facts through candidate
retrieval, which misses some paraphrases and many pronoun references
(Section~\ref{sec:results-main}); the seeded fixtures were written by the same LLM
annotator. An implied verdict judges what a reader would infer, so the line between an
implied disclosure and an eligible prediction rests on the annotator. A zero count
therefore does not certify spoiler-free output, and \sys's larger output leaves more room
for missed disclosures.

\paragraph{Scope of the comparisons.}
The accumulation gains are measured against pages written in one call under a sentence
budget, while \sys renders every eligible claim; longer, multi-call or append-only pages may
narrow them, and no length-matched control was run. The central/other split was chosen
after the results were known, and facts lost across cutoffs also count labeling
inconsistencies between adjacent cutoffs. Identity-dependence rates compare single builds,
so they include stochastic model decisions as well as later-volume effects. B4 differs
from \sys in page scope and generation as well as citation attachment, and neither the
account of Section~\ref{sec:discussion} nor the proposed repairs have been tested by
ablation.

\paragraph{The treated text.}
Renaming and paraphrase reduce recognition without removing it. The treated text was not
proofread against its source: some paragraphs keep inflected source names, name collisions
create namesakes, and one paraphrase changes a fact (Appendix~\ref{app:contamination}).
Its fidelity to the gold facts was checked by an unblinded LLM. A work published after the
generator's training cutoff would offer a complementary test of recognition
\citep{karpinska2024nocha}.

\paragraph{Representation and readers.}
Cutoffs are whole volumes; identity links are not dated; attribute intervals track
explicit replacements rather than implicit aging; a value that changes within one volume
is arbitrated as an extraction conflict and one value is published
(Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}); and cross-claim inference is limited
(Appendix~\ref{app:observations}). We do not measure readers' ability to find information
or their experience of spoilers. External search engines and deliberately opened later
cutoffs are outside the audited artifact.
