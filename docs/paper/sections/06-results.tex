\paragraph{Interpreting the comparisons.}
These are single-run, LLM-labeled pilot results. Claude finalized all AI labels and
also assisted in implementation; the AI judgments were not blinded to system identity.
\ifnwhuman{} Human checks covered selected original-text items, excluding
disclosure and treated conditions; review disagreements did not uniformly correct system
scores.\fi{} Judgment rules, review coverage and sampling can still affect comparisons
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}; Appendix~\ref{app:annotation}).

\subsection{Disclosure of later facts}
\label{sec:results-main}
B1, B2 and B3 each have no stated or implied disclosures in 220 fact--cutoff assessments;
\sys has one implied disclosure (Table~\ref{tab:main}). At $t=3$, a relationship summary
says a friend \emph{later serves as a bridesmaid for Anne}, implying Anne's wedding. The
eligible passage instead describes Anne as the friend's bridesmaid: the generator inverted
the relation. Withholding later text therefore reduces an exposure channel without
preventing disclosure through incorrect inference. B4 also has no confirmed disclosure.
The diagnostics disclose more: X1 states \res{M4-anne-total-X1} later facts without source text,
and X2 states or implies \res{M4-anne-total-X2} despite its cutoff instruction.

\paragraph{Detection limits and uncertain cases.}
Including uncertain verdicts raises the count for the five input-bounded systems to
\res{M4-anne-total-cut-incl}: \sys has five of 220, while X2 has 14. \sys's four uncertain
verdicts arise from a relation labeled \emph{romantically involved with} from volume one
(Appendix~\ref{app:casebook}). The candidate search retrieves \res{SEED-para} of seeded
paraphrases naming the character and \res{SEED-oblique} of seeded implications; with
pronouns on another character's page, retrieval falls to roughly one third
(Table~\ref{tab:seeds}). These are detection-limited counts on a finite probe set.
At $t=2$, \sys contains \res{N-anne-t2-B5} assertions versus B1's
\res{N-anne-t2-B1}; unequal artifact sizes further complicate comparisons of missed
disclosures. Facts also cluster within characters and cutoffs. M4 excludes identity
effects, which we examine separately in Section~\ref{sec:results-scope}.

\paragraph{Checks independent of candidate retrieval.}
Among sampled assertions at $t\le4$, none in \sys, B1, B2 or B3 is established only by later
volumes (\sys: \res{M5-anne-B5}; B3: \res{M5-anne-B3}), versus \res{M5-anne-X2} for X2
and \res{M5-anne-X1} for X1. A lexical screen finds one name absent from $\Dt$ on each
input-bounded system's pages, including \sys, versus \res{SCREEN-anne-X1} for X1 and
\res{SCREEN-anne-X2} for X2. The input-bounded hits are a plural or an expanded title,
rather than confirmed later-character disclosures. Both checks have limited coverage:
the assertion samples are small, and the screen detects only new names.

\subsection{Coverage, support and citations}
\label{sec:results-quality}
\paragraph{Coverage.}
Closed-book recall of \respt{M1-anne-avg-X1} identifies recognition as a substantial
confound; exceeding this diagnostic does not isolate the contribution of reading.
\sys has lower recall than long-context prompting at every cutoff
(\respt{M1-anne-avg-B5} versus \respt{M1-anne-avg-B1}; Figure~\ref{fig:cutoffplot}).
Its mean paired difference relative to prefix retrieval is
\respt{M1-anne-avg-B5-minus-B2}. Table~\ref{tab:paired} gives the other comparisons.
\ifnwdecon Section~\ref{sec:results-decon} tests the pattern after reducing recognition.\fi{}

\paragraph{Support and publication scope.}
Whole-artifact precision is \respt{M3-anne-avg-B5} for \sys, versus
\respt{M3-anne-avg-B1}, \respt{M3-anne-avg-B2} and \respt{M3-anne-avg-B3} for B1--B3.
On cast character pages, the matched population, \sys reaches \res{M3K-anne-B5-cast},
versus B1's \res{M3K-anne-B1}; the \sys estimate rests on only 35 atoms.
The largest gap occurs on relationship pages (\res{M3K-anne-B5-rel-share} of its
assertions): \res{M3K-anne-B5-rel} are supported in page context, whereas
\res{M3K-anne-B5-rel-atom} are supported as statements alone. Relationship pages include
lines from chapter-level scene records naming both characters, even when the two do
not share an episode. Under the \emph{line rule}, precision is \res{REV-M3L}
(Table~\ref{tab:builds}). \sys also publishes \res{ND-anne-avg-B5} distinct assertions per
cutoff, nearly four times B1's \res{ND-anne-avg-B1}; recall on a fixed gold inventory
does not measure this additional content.

\paragraph{Citation sufficiency.}
Paragraph-citation coverage, measured over each full assertion inventory and averaged
across cutoffs, is \res{CITE-anne-B5} for \sys (B1: \res{CITE-anne-B1}). This differs from
sampled citation sufficiency, whose cutoff mean is \respt{M8-anne-avg-B5}, versus
\respt{M8-anne-avg-B1} for B1 and \respt{M8-anne-avg-B2} for B2.
After excluding one enumeration error, \sys has 199 evaluated sampled atoms: 191 carry
citations and 102 are fully established
by them. Restricting the pooled sample denominator to cited atoms yields
\res{M8C-anne-B5}; the corresponding values are \res{M8C-anne-B1} for B1 and
\res{M8C-anne-B2} for B2. Citation omission alone therefore does not explain the gap:
evidence selection or attachment also falls short.

\paragraph{Control using the same graph.}
B4 uses \sys's graph but generates only cast character pages from retrieved facts.
It has slightly lower recall (\respt{M1-anne-avg-B4} versus \respt{M1-anne-avg-B5}),
but higher precision (\respt{M3-anne-avg-B4}) and citation sufficiency
(\respt{M8-anne-avg-B4}). Together with the page-type breakdown, this control implicates
publication context and citation attachment in the quality gap; it does not isolate
their separate effects (Appendix~\ref{app:additional}). The reported \sys is its second
build, revised after first-build labeling; Table~\ref{tab:builds} reports both.

\subsection{Does construction from later volumes matter?}
\label{sec:results-scope}
Using the first build's code, we constructed graphs from volumes $1..2$ and $1..3$
and compared their wikis with the corresponding cutoffs of the full-series build.
Neither disclosed an assessed later fact (0 of 65 at $t=2$, 0 of 45 at $t=3$), and
recall and sampled precision were close (Table~\ref{tab:scope}).

The first build's entity inventories nevertheless differ. Of the \res{A1-t2-onesided} character pages
present in only one build at $t=2$, \res{A1-t2-anchored} are anchored to later characters
(\res{A1-t3-anchored} at $t=3$) but display an earlier namesake. For example, the
first full-series build's \texttt{walter} entity is dominated by Walter Blythe from volume five,
with 127 of its 130 mentions; its cutoff-2 page instead describes Anne's father.
The page's existence and identity depend on unavailable text even without a later-fact
disclosure.

For the reported build, comparing full-series entities with prefix-discovered indexes
gives identity dependence of \res{ID-anne-t1-rate} at $t=1$ and
\res{ID-anne-t4-rate} at $t=4$. Here dependence means that no prefix-built character
has exactly the page entity's set of names observed by $t$; it includes changed alias
links, regrouping and absent counterparts (Table~\ref{tab:identity}). Wikis built on
those prefix indexes match the full build's pooled recall
(\res{PX-anne-M1-pooled} each, $t=1..4$) and state no later fact
(Table~\ref{tab:prefixv2}). Thus content filtering alone does not make entity
construction independent of later volumes.
Archived early-volume prompts also expose later aliases such as ``Mistress Blythe'' and
``Mrs. Doctor'' (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}). The index and wiki comparisons do not isolate
the influence of this prompt-metadata channel.
