**Recommendation: major revision; presently weak reject for a regular COLING/NAACL main-conference research paper.** The topic fits both venues. ## What works

The reader-relative task is useful and well motivated. Publication-order cutoffs, rather than story chronology, are a sensible definition. The distinction between a fact being true, available to the reader, and sufficiently cited is worth preserving. Evaluating navigation, titles, relationships, shared pages and citation source views is stronger than evaluating only generated paragraphs.

The design is inspectable: stable evidence identifiers, explicit intervals, deterministic fields, filtering before page prose, and logged/cached calls. The casebook shows concrete failures that ordinary retrieval-only evaluations overlook, particularly later-anchored identities and predictions flattened into facts.

The paper reports its own losses rather than hiding them. On the original text NarrativeWiki's selected-gold recall is 0.802 versus long context's 0.848, and citation sufficiency is 0.512 versus 0.658. Those are useful negative findings. A method need not beat every baseline to merit publication; a reliable new evaluation or a well-established failure mechanism can be enough. The problem here is whether the measurements and general conclusions have been validated sufficiently.

## Issues most likely to determine acceptance

### 1. The human review found disagreements that still feed the published metrics

This is the most urgent issue. Appendix B, physical pages 14–15 (`appendix/a-annotation.tex:162–182`), says accepted review answers agree with only **120/148 precision labels** and **46/60 citation labels**. It then explains that the sampled precision/citation labels were left unchanged, to avoid correcting only one tenth of the population. The local human-review record corroborates that some precision answers became partial under the accepted reading.

Avoiding a sample-only correction is reasonable. It does not make the old population estimates validated. The right next step is to propagate the accepted rules to the affected populations, or publish separate audited-sample results and a clear sensitivity analysis. The present headline M3/M8 remain estimates from the earlier LLM labels, despite observed disagreements. Their numerical bias cannot be calculated simply by subtracting the sample disagreements: the direction and system distribution require analysis.

The phrase “sample human-reviewed” is literally supported, but readers may interpret it as validation of the reported metrics. State the disagreement in the main paper and distinguish review of suggested answers from independent annotation. Do not advertise post-review numbers without saying which review findings changed those numbers and which did not.

### 2. The disclosure search is much less sensitive in an important output setting

The main text foregrounds search recall around 0.845 for named paraphrases and 0.714 for named implications. **Table 10, page 21, also reports 68/220 (0.309) and 78/220 (0.355) when a pronoun occurs on another character's page.** That is directly relevant to NarrativeWiki's relationship pages and other shared surfaces.

Only retrieved sentences are judged, and no human reviewed disclosure verdicts. Thus “0 detected disclosures” does not establish “no spoilers.” Search failures can differ by system because their outputs differ in size, pronoun use and page structure. The finite future-gold inventory is also not a complete spoiler inventory. The paper acknowledges these limitations, which is good, but the least favourable detector condition belongs in the main results, not only the appendix.

Use an independently validated challenge set, judge blinded positive and negative cases, and read a random sample of outputs not retrieved by the search. Report sensitivity by surface/wording and system. A better lexical search alone does not validate the annotator's interpretation of implication or the absence of spoilers outside the probe set.

### 3. Annotator identity is confounded with system and text condition

Original-text precision/citation labels for NarrativeWiki come from a different agent than the comparison systems. A builder/annotator supplies relationship-pair verdicts. On treated text one annotator labels everything. Neither annotation pass is blind to system identity, and the reported audits initially found harsher treatment of comparison systems. Rechecking only negative labels also leaves positive-label errors unaudited.

These are disclosed methodological limitations, not accusations of misconduct. Nevertheless, they prevent clean attribution of some quality differences to the systems. Use the same independently assigned, blinded human evaluation protocol across systems and text conditions, with overlapping assignments and adjudication. Report role, book familiarity, time, compensation and consent appropriately; the current review notes say those details are still outstanding.

### 4. One development series does not establish general performance

Five cutoffs are dependent views of one series, not five independent narrative corpora. The submitted system was revised after seeing labels on that series, while the baselines did not receive an equivalent revision. Every book fits within the generator's context, so the supposed structural advantages on genuinely longer works remain untested. One model and one build do not estimate generation variability.

The treated Anne text is another condition of the same story, not a held-out narrative. Freeze the system and evaluation before testing an independent work within the maintainer-authorised corpus scope. Repeat generation only where variability could change the interpretation of a small effect. Until that work is done, keep the claims explicitly case-study scoped.

### 5. The treatment reduces recognition but does not isolate memory causally

The closed-book collapse from 0.683 to 0.003 is strong evidence that recognisability matters for that diagnostic. It does not quantify the memory contribution to each reading system.

The treatment still has 0.110 title recognition, retained source-name surfaces, name collisions and at least one factual mutation. Appendix K describes a children's-age answer becoming minutes; a system is then counted as supported when it reproduces the altered text. The treatment was not proofread against the original, and the gold-fidelity check did not repeat the stricter evidence-sufficiency test. The names-only recognition probe is helpful, but names-only generation results are not reported for the system matrix.

Use “recognition-reducing treatment” or “stress test” rather than claiming to separate reading from memory. Validate fidelity, coreference and modality before freezing a revised treatment. If fixing the text/map, invalidate and regenerate affected cells; do not present old runs as results on a corrected treatment.

### 6. The headline systems publish different kinds of artifacts

Baselines get the evaluated cast and write character pages. NarrativeWiki discovers its own cast and adds relationship, codex, timeline and other pages. Whole-artifact metrics describe the delivered products, but they do not isolate graph extraction from roster discovery or page rendering.

Extra surfaces provide extra recall opportunities and extra opportunities for precision errors. The shared-cast comparison has only 35 sampled NarrativeWiki atoms; citation sufficiency there is 14/35. That is too small to support a claim of equivalence. Show matched cast/surface comparisons with enough stratified samples alongside whole-artifact measurements. Treat discovery quality as a separate outcome. The same-graph B4 diagnostic is useful and deserves more main-paper prominence.

## Novelty and presentation

Temporal filtering, character representation, source attribution and spoiler-aware access all have prior work. The manuscript correctly avoids claiming temporal filtering itself is new. The stronger novelty case is the combination of reader-positioned delivered artifacts, identity/construction threats, surface-wide assessment and a reusable evaluation protocol.

However, that makes instrument validation central. A protocol with unvalidated LLM labels and poor cross-page recall is not yet a strong benchmark contribution. Strengthen those parts before adding pipeline complexity. The title currently foregrounds a system; consider framing the paper around evaluating reader-cutoff references and using NarrativeWiki as the case study.

[CHIRON](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.499/) is a close representation/verification comparison worth adapting, or explicitly explaining why it cannot be compared under the common task. This is a useful scientific suggestion, not a claim that every related paper is a mandatory baseline. The recent SearchWiki and NS-ST-GraphRAG preprints should be discussed, but their recency alone does not mandate new experiments before submission.

The main prose is generally clear but dense with metric codes and repeated caveats. Give the principal definitions once, use descriptive metric names in running text, and retain necessary uncertainty next to each claim. Key treatment, detector and matched-surface comparisons are easier to assess if a compact summary is in the main paper. Move verbose logged exchanges to supplementary files if useful; a long appendix is permitted but should not be needed to discover a central validity limitation. Check small diagram text at printed A4 size. Figure 1's design promise should match the candid prose.

## Suitability and submission mechanics

The task fits generation, retrieval, factuality, evaluation and digital-humanities areas in the official [COLING 2027 call](https://2027.coling-iccl.org/calls/main_conference_papers/) and [NAACL 2027 call](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/main_conference_papers/). Neither venue requires the special theme. NAACL's agentic-communication theme is not a natural fit merely because agents helped build or label the system.

Both calls specify **October 12, 2026** for the October ARR submission and **December 23, 2026** for commitment. The ARR submission is conference-agnostic; select one primary venue at commitment. Optional consideration by the other venue is capacity-dependent, not guaranteed. The eight-page content limit is met by this snapshot. Limitations is present; appendices/references can extend beyond it. These are format checks, not evidence of acceptance readiness.

The supplied reading PDF deliberately suppresses line numbers. Submit the separate line-numbered `dist/NarrativeWiki-paper-submission.pdf`, and verify it again after editing. The [ACL formatting guidelines](https://acl-org.github.io/ACLPUB/formatting.html) require the review ruler and an abstract of at most 200 words. The [ARR call](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp) requires anonymity of supplementary artifacts and a truthful Responsible NLP checklist. The paper contains an anonymous repository URL; its actual external contents/accessibility were not exhaustively audited here. Confirm that the intended frozen material is available to reviewers. Do not count dormant camera-ready author/funding placeholders as missing anonymous-review content.

`paper_status.py` returns a brace error in the literal prompt appendix: its simple checker counts truncated JSON braces inside 53 `lstlisting` blocks. Outside those blocks braces balance. That result should not be reported as a failed manuscript compilation; this review did not run a fresh LaTeX build.

A separate demo paper is conceivable if the goal is demonstrating a usable tool. It requires the relevant demo-track design, empirical assessment and submission materials; it does not cure these evidence problems or make acceptance automatic. A narrower negative-results/evaluation paper is also defensible, provided its central findings are independently checked. The [ARR review form](https://aclrollingreview.org/reviewform) explicitly distinguishes conference novelty/impact from Findings' emphasis on soundness and reproducibility.
